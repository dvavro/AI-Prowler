🔒 Data Privacy & Confidentiality

Handling customer and company data responsibly is part of every role here. This training covers what counts as sensitive data, how to protect it, and what to do if something goes wrong.

⏱ 20 min 📋 Required 🔒 HIPAA / Privacy
Course Progress0%

1 Why This Matters

Every day, you handle information that customers and coworkers trust AI-Prowler to protect — names, contact details, payment information, and sometimes health or other sensitive personal data. Mishandling that data isn't just a mistake; it can cause real harm to real people, and expose the company to legal liability.

🚨 A data privacy violation — even an accidental one — must be reported. Trying to quietly fix it yourself is itself a policy violation.

2 What Counts as Sensitive Data

🪪

Personal Info (PII)

Names, addresses, phone numbers, emails, Social Security numbers, dates of birth.

💳

Financial Data

Payment card numbers, bank account details, payroll and compensation info.

🏥

Health Information

Any medical, health, or disability-related information about a customer or employee.

🔑

Login Credentials

Passwords, API keys, session tokens — anyone's, including your own.

📊

Business Data

Client lists, pricing, contracts, and internal strategy — confidential to AI-Prowler.

👤

Employee Records

Coworkers' HR files, performance reviews, and personal details.

3 The Rules for Handling Data

  • Need-to-know only: Only access data that's actually required for your role and current task.
  • Company systems only: Never store customer or company data on personal devices, personal cloud accounts, or personal email.
  • No casual sharing: Never discuss specific customer or employee data in public channels, social media, or outside conversations.
  • Lock it up: Lock your screen whenever you step away, even at home.
  • Minimum necessary: When sharing data internally, share only what the other person actually needs.
📎 If you're ever unsure whether you're allowed to access or share something, don't guess — ask HR first.

4 Account & Password Security

  • Use a unique, strong password for every work account — never reuse passwords across sites
  • Never share your login credentials with anyone, including coworkers or your manager
  • Enable two-factor authentication wherever it's offered
  • Never write passwords down somewhere others could find them

5 Recognizing Phishing & Social Engineering

Attackers often try to trick employees into handing over access, rather than breaking in directly. Watch for:

  • Urgent, pressuring emails or messages asking you to click a link or "verify" your account
  • Requests that seem to come from a manager or executive but feel slightly off
  • Login pages that look almost right, but the web address doesn't quite match
  • Anyone — internal or external — asking for your password "just this once"
🎣 When in doubt, don't click. Report suspicious messages to HR immediately instead of investigating them yourself.

6 If You Suspect a Data Breach

  1. Stop — don't try to fix it or cover it up on your own
  2. Report it to HR immediately via the Messages tab or Incident Reports tab
  3. Note what happened, when, and what data may have been involved
  4. Follow any instructions HR gives you — do not discuss it outside the investigation
⏱ A data breach must be reported within hours, not days. Every hour of delay increases potential harm to the people affected.

7 Confidentiality After Employment

Your confidentiality and NDA obligations don't end when you leave AI-Prowler. Customer data, business strategy, and internal information you learned here must stay confidential — permanently, not just while you're employed.
