🤝 Anti-Harassment & Discrimination

This training covers AI-Prowler's zero-tolerance policy on harassment and discrimination, how to recognize prohibited behavior, what to do if you experience or witness it, and your rights as an employee.

1 Why This Training Matters

AI-Prowler has a zero-tolerance policy for harassment and discrimination. This isn't just about legal compliance — it's about creating a workplace where every single person feels safe, respected, and valued, regardless of who they are or where they work from.

As a remote company, our interactions happen through screens — but that does not make harassment or discrimination any less real or harmful. A hostile message, an exclusionary comment on a video call, or a discriminatory joke in a chat thread can cause just as much harm as if it happened in person.

🚨 Violations of this policy can result in immediate termination — and in serious cases, legal action. There are no exceptions and no second chances for severe violations.

3 What Is Harassment?

Harassment is any unwelcome conduct based on a protected characteristic that creates a hostile, intimidating, or offensive work environment — OR that results in an adverse employment decision (like being fired or demoted).

Types of Harassment

  • Verbal: Slurs, offensive jokes, derogatory comments, name-calling, threats
  • Written/Digital: Offensive emails, chats, messages, memes, or social media posts directed at a coworker
  • Visual: Displaying offensive images, symbols, or content in a shared video call background or shared screen
  • Physical: Unwanted touching (at in-person events), invasion of personal space
  • Cyberbullying: Repeated hostile or humiliating messages sent through work or personal channels
📌 Example — Harassment

A team member sends a chat message with a derogatory joke about a colleague's accent. Even if it was "just a joke" and even if it was sent privately, this is harassment based on national origin. It is prohibited and reportable.

4 Sexual Harassment

Sexual harassment is one of the most common forms of workplace harassment and deserves specific attention. It falls into two categories:

Quid Pro Quo

"This for that" — when someone in a position of power makes employment decisions (hiring, promotion, assignments) contingent on sexual favors or submission to unwanted sexual conduct.

📌 Example

A manager implies to a direct report that getting a promotion depends on going on a date with them. This is quid pro quo sexual harassment — a serious violation that would result in immediate termination.

Hostile Work Environment

When unwelcome sexual conduct is so severe or pervasive that it creates an intimidating, hostile, or offensive work environment. This includes:

  • Repeated unwanted compliments about physical appearance
  • Sending sexually suggestive messages, images, or GIFs
  • Making sexual jokes or innuendo in team chats or calls
  • Asking intrusive questions about someone's personal or romantic life
  • Discussing sexual topics in work communication channels
🔞 Sharing, displaying, or sending sexually explicit content through any work channel is grounds for immediate termination.

5 What Is Discrimination?

Discrimination is treating someone differently — or less favorably — in employment decisions because of a protected characteristic. Employment decisions include hiring, firing, pay, job assignments, promotions, layoffs, training, and benefits.

📌 Example — Discrimination

An employee is passed over for a promotion despite being the most qualified candidate. Later it's revealed that the hiring manager made the decision based on the employee's religion. This is illegal religious discrimination.

Disparate Treatment vs. Disparate Impact

  • Disparate Treatment: Intentionally treating someone differently because of a protected characteristic
  • Disparate Impact: A neutral policy that, in practice, disproportionately harms a protected group — even if unintentional

7 What To Do If It Happens To You

If you experience harassment or discrimination at AI-Prowler — even if you're not sure it meets the legal definition — you have the right to report it. Here's what to do:

  1. Document it. Save the message, screenshot the conversation, note the date, time, and any witnesses. Keep this documentation somewhere personal and secure.
  2. You don't have to confront the person. You are never required to address the harasser directly. Go straight to HR if you prefer.
  3. Report it to HR. Use the Messages tab or Incident Reports tab in the HR portal. All reports are confidential.
  4. HR will investigate. AI-Prowler will investigate every report promptly, fairly, and confidentially. You will be kept informed of the outcome.
🔒 You will NEVER face retaliation for reporting harassment or discrimination in good faith. Retaliation is itself a serious policy violation.

8 What To Do If You Witness It

Bystanders play a crucial role in preventing harassment. If you witness harassment or discrimination:

  • Speak up if it's safe to do so. A simple "That's not appropriate" or "Let's keep things professional" can be powerful.
  • Support the target privately. Check in with the person who was targeted. Let them know you saw what happened and that you support them.
  • Report it to HR. You don't have to be the direct target to report. Witnesses are encouraged to report what they saw.
  • Document what you observed. Note the date, time, what was said, and who was present.
👁 Silence in the face of harassment can be interpreted as approval. You have a responsibility to your colleagues to speak up or report.

9 Retaliation — What It Is and Why It's Prohibited

Retaliation occurs when someone takes a negative action against an employee because they reported harassment, filed a complaint, or participated in an investigation. Retaliation is illegal and is a separate, serious violation of AI-Prowler policy.

Retaliation can include:

  • Demotion, termination, or reduced hours
  • Exclusion from meetings, projects, or communications
  • Negative performance reviews not based on actual performance
  • Hostile treatment, cold-shoulder behavior, or ostracism
  • Threats, intimidation, or spreading rumors
⚖️ If you experience retaliation after making a report, report the retaliation immediately to HR. It will be treated as a separate, serious violation.
