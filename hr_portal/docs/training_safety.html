🦺 Workplace Safety & OSHA Basics

This training covers essential workplace safety principles, OSHA regulations, hazard identification, and emergency procedures for AI-Prowler's remote work environment.

1 Why Workplace Safety Matters — Even at Home

Workplace safety isn't just for factories and construction sites. As a remote employee, your home is your workplace, and AI-Prowler is committed to ensuring you have a safe and healthy work environment — wherever that is.

OSHA (Occupational Safety and Health Administration) is the federal agency that sets and enforces workplace safety standards. While OSHA's jurisdiction over home offices is limited, AI-Prowler voluntarily upholds OSHA principles to protect every team member.

📋 Under workers' compensation law, injuries that occur during work hours at your home office may be covered — which means workplace safety at home is a real legal and practical concern.

2 Your Home Office Safety Checklist

Before we dive in, review this checklist for your home workspace:

  • My workspace is free from clutter, trip hazards, and obstructions
  • Electrical cords are managed and not a trip or fire hazard
  • My workspace is well-lit to reduce eye strain
  • I have a working smoke detector in or near my workspace
  • My computer and equipment are on a surge protector
  • My chair provides proper back support
  • My monitor is at eye level and arm's length away
  • I take regular breaks to stretch and rest my eyes
  • I know the location of my nearest fire extinguisher
  • I have a basic first aid kit accessible at home

4 Ergonomics — Preventing Repetitive Strain

Ergonomic injuries are the #1 work-from-home safety issue. Hours of poor posture can lead to back pain, carpal tunnel syndrome, and neck strain that can become chronic. Follow these guidelines:

Body PartCorrect PositionCommon Mistake
BackUpright with lumbar support, feet flat on floorSlouching, sitting on a couch or bed
Arms/WristsElbows at 90°, wrists straight while typingWrists bent up or down, arms reaching forward
MonitorTop of screen at or below eye level, arm's length awayScreen too low (looking down) or too high
NeckHead balanced over shoulders, not jutting forwardCraning forward to see screen
Eyes20-20-20 rule: every 20 min, look 20 ft away for 20 secStaring at screen for hours without breaks
💪 Stand up and stretch every 60-90 minutes. Set a timer if needed. Your body will thank you — and so will your productivity.

5 Emergency Procedures

Even at home, emergencies happen. Know what to do before they do.

🔥 Fire

  • If you smell smoke or see fire — evacuate immediately. Don't stop to save equipment.
  • Call 911 from outside the building.
  • Do not re-enter until cleared by fire department.
  • Notify HR via the portal as soon as you are safe.

⚡ Power Outage

  • Save your work immediately if you get a warning (UPS/surge protector).
  • Notify your manager via phone or text that you are experiencing an outage.
  • Log the outage in the Time Clock as an approved interruption.
  • Work may continue from a safe alternate location (library, coffee shop with VPN).

🏥 Medical Emergency

  • Call 911 immediately for any life-threatening emergency.
  • Know the address of your nearest emergency room.
  • If you are injured during work hours, file an Incident Report in the HR portal as soon as possible.
🚨 For ANY work-related injury or illness — no matter how minor — file an Incident Report in the HR portal within 24 hours. This protects both you and AI-Prowler legally.

7 Cybersecurity as a Safety Issue

In a remote environment, cybersecurity is a workplace safety issue. A data breach can harm you, your colleagues, and AI-Prowler's customers. Basic safety rules:

  • Use a password-protected Wi-Fi network — never work on open public Wi-Fi without a VPN.
  • Lock your screen whenever you step away — even at home.
  • Never leave sensitive documents visible on screen in shared spaces.
  • Report suspicious emails, links, or login attempts to HR immediately.
  • Keep your devices updated — security patches fix known vulnerabilities.

8 Mental Health & Wellbeing

Workplace safety includes mental health. Remote work can lead to isolation, burnout, and stress that — left unaddressed — can be just as harmful as physical injury.

  • Set boundaries: Stop working at the end of your scheduled shift. Unplug.
  • Take your PTO: Vacation time exists to rest and recharge. Use it.
  • Stay connected: Reach out to colleagues. Isolation is a real risk of remote work.
  • Ask for help: If you're struggling, message HR confidentially. We're here for you.
❤️ If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) — available 24/7. For domestic violence support: 1-800-799-7233.
