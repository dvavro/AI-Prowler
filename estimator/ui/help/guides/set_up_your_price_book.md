# Set up your price book
<!-- screen: price_book; words: prices, cost, sample, import, supplier, csv, rates, price book, history, markups, tax, columns -->
Each trade and service comes with sample prices so you can try it out. Put in your own costs before you send real quotes.

1. Open **Price Book** in the left menu (owners and estimators).
2. Tick **Samples only** to see the prices you haven't set yet. Pick a trade or service under **Show**, or a **Kind** (material, labor...), to narrow the list.
3. Type your cost per unit in the **Cost per unit** box. Changed boxes are highlighted.
4. Optionally pick the date the new prices **Take effect**.
5. Click **Save changes…**, check the list of old → new prices, then click **Yes, save**.

## Import a supplier price list
1. Copy the rows from your supplier's spreadsheet, header row first.
2. Open **Import a supplier price list (paste CSV)** at the bottom and paste them in.
3. Click **1. Check the columns…**. Each column is matched by its name (item code, description, unit, cost); change any that are wrong, or pick **(none)**.
4. Click **2. Check the list…**. It shows what will change and which rows were skipped (for example a different unit).
5. Click **Yes, save** to import.

## Price history and markups
- Click **📈 History** on a price to see every dated price it has had, how many estimates used it, and what jobs really paid (from the job costs recorded after each job). When two or more jobs show your price is 10% or more off, it says so.
- The line at the top shows what's added on top of these costs: overhead, profit, labor burden, tax and the minimum charge. The owner changes them in **Settings → Tax, markups and labor**.

## Good to know
- Changing a price never changes a quote that's already been made.
- Rows with a different unit are skipped, never converted.
